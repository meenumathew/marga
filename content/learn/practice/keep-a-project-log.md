# Keep A Project Log

This is a hands-on walkthrough. By the end you will have produced one small output and logged it as evidence, so the habit is real and not just an idea you read.

Pick something you can finish in one sitting. A tiny script, a single refactor, a one-page design note. Small is the point.

## Step 1: Name the work

Write one sentence: what you are going to make and why it matters. For example, "Refactor the parser so the validation and the transform steps are separate, because they change for different reasons."

If you cannot write that sentence, the task is too vague. Shrink it until you can.

## Step 2: Do the work

Build the thing. Keep the scope fixed. When you notice a tempting side quest, write it down for later and stay on the sentence from Step 1.

## Step 3: Capture the before and after

For a refactor, save the shape of the code before and after. For a build, note what did not exist before and what runs now. Two or three lines is enough. This is the raw material for explaining the work later.

## Step 4: Get one piece of feedback

Show the output to one person and ask a specific question. Not "what do you think" but "does this boundary make the next change easier or harder?" Specific questions get useful answers. Write down what they said.

## Step 5: Log it as evidence

Open the [Evidence](/evidence) page and add two entries:

1. The output itself. Pick the kind (Build, Refactor, Writeup, and so on), give it a title, and attach it to the section it belongs to.
2. The feedback. Pick the Feedback kind and record who gave it and what they said.

## What you have now

You produced something, you heard how it landed, and both are logged. Section mastery and the scorecard now reflect real work. Repeat this loop with the next small task, and the log becomes the record of how you actually got better.
